# Philosophical Field Map

Use when the subject is defined by tensions, assumptions, tradeoffs, or hidden conceptual terrain.

## Visual System

- Structure: field map with axes, regions, and boundary lines
- Composition: one large field, two main axes, sparse nodes, marginal annotations
- Style terms: monochrome blueprint, fine linework, paper, ambient light, monotone
- Palette: graphite, off-white, muted blue-gray
- Text policy: region names and axis labels only; avoid sentence labels

## Prompt Template

```text
Create a philosophical field map about {subject}. Show the core tensions as two crossing axes, named regions, sparse idea nodes, and a few thin boundary lines. Use monochrome blueprint style, fine analytic linework, paper texture, ambient light, restrained annotations, quiet intellectual mood. Make the relationships inspectable rather than decorative. No fantasy map, no neon, no motivational slogans, no crowded infographic cards.
```

## Good Fit

- Expert interviews about principles
- Strategy, ethics, psychology, culture, or philosophy
- Visuals where the reader should decide where to dive
